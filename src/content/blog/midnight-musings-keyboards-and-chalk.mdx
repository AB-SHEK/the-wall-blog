Have you ever looked closely at the desk of an engineer who also boulders four evenings a week? 

There is a fine, imperceptible dusting of magnesium carbonate settled into the crevices of an anodized aluminum custom mechanical keyboard. It is unavoidable. No amount of hand scrubbing completely rids your pores of chalk when you've just come back from a three-hour moonboard session.

<PullQuote author="Anonymous Crag Hacker">
"Both hobbies involve intense obsession with millimeters, tactile feedback, and spending unreasonable amounts of money on tiny colorful plastic pieces."
</PullQuote>

## The Shared Psychology of Keyboards & Climbing

Consider the parallels:

| Bouldering Aspect | Mechanical Keyboard Equivalent |
|---|---|
| **Hold Texture** | Keycap Profile (PBT dye-sub vs. ABS double-shot) |
| **Edge Depth (10mm crimp)** | Switch Pre-Travel (1.8mm actuation) |
| **Body Tension** | Ergonomic Split Layout (Colemak-DH) |
| **Chalk & Friction** | Krytox 205g0 Switch Lube & Poron Foam |

<Callout type="warning" title="Keyboard Maintenance Warning">
Do not blow compressed air directly into switch stems if you have climbing chalk on your keyboard plate. You will simply bake the chalk into the slider rails, creating the world's scratchiest 80-grit linear switch!
</Callout>

At the end of the day, both pursuits celebrate the joy of human fingertips making tactile, intentional contact with physical surfaces.
