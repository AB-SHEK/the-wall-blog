Every climber knows the feeling: standing at the base of a boulder problem, chalk bag dipped, chalk dust suspended in the dry gym or crisp mountain air. You look up, trace the sequence of holds with your fingertips, and visualize every single body tension shift before your feet even leave the crash pad.

<PullQuote author="Wolfgang Güllich">
"The brain is the most important muscle for climbing."
</PullQuote>

When I set out to redesign my personal website, standard flat scroll documents felt inadequate. Why should navigating between thoughts, travel memories, and research feel like flipping generic PDF pages when it could feel like **moving up a wall**?

## The Anatomy of the Route

On this blog:
1. **Sections are routes**: From Base Camp up to the highest crags.
2. **Holds represent links**: Jugs for deep comfortable topics, razor crimps for intense technical deep dives, and slopers for wide-open musings.
3. **The active hold is chalked**: Leaving an unmistakable mark of where your hands just gripped.

<Callout type="beta" title="How Navigation Works">
Clicking a link reaches up or down the wall. As your avatar grabs the target hold, chalk puffs explode, and the wall shifts beneath your feet with tactile, eased inertia.
</Callout>

### What You'll Find Here

- **Expeditions**: Stories from remote boulders across fontainebleau, granite valleys, and limestone roofs.
- **The Beta Library**: Biomechanical research, finger pulley dynamics, and book summaries.
- **Random Sends**: Web experiments, mechanical key switches, and spontaneous late-night essays.

Grab your chalk bag, pick a hold, and let's send.
